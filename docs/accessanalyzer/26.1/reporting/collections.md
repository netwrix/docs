A collection is a folder for questions, dashboards, and other collections. You pick one when you save, and you can move items between collections later from each item's menu.

## Kinds of Collections

| Collection | Who can open it | Who can change it |
|---|---|---|
| Your personal collection, named after you | Only you | Only you |
| Shared collections under **Our analytics** | Everyone who can use Reporting | Everyone who can use Reporting |
| **Netwrix reports**, **Home**, and **Deprecated** | Everyone who can use Reporting | Nobody |

Nobody else can open your personal collection, Admins included. Use it for drafts and for work only you need.

Shared collections are for work your team uses together. Anyone who can use Reporting can create a collection under **Our analytics**, save to it, and edit, move, or delete what's in it, including items someone else saved. For example, create a collection named `Finance audit` and save the questions and dashboards the finance review needs there. Anyone who can use Reporting can also restore an item that someone moved from a shared collection to the trash.

## The Read-Only Collections

Three collections ship with Access Analyzer. You can open everything in them, but nobody can save to them or change, move, or delete what they hold.

- **Netwrix reports** holds the dashboards described in [Netwrix reports](netwrix-reports.md).
- **Home** holds the questions behind the tiles on the Access Analyzer Home page.
- **Deprecated** holds Netwrix questions that an upgrade retired while your own questions or dashboards still used them. Access Analyzer moves them here instead of deleting them, so your work keeps running. When nothing uses one anymore, a later upgrade removes it.

## When Someone Leaves

When an Admin deactivates or deletes a user in Access Analyzer, nobody can open that person's personal collection anymore. Before that happens, ask the person to move anything the team needs into a shared collection. Items the person saved in shared collections stay where they are.
